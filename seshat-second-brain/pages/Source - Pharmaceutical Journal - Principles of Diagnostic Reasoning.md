## Key Insights
- **Dual-Process Cognitive Model:** Diagnostic reasoning operates on two systems: System 1 (fast, intuitive, pattern-based) and System 2 (slow, analytical, rule-based). Balancing both is key to both efficiency and accuracy.
- **Hypothetico-Deductive Framework:** System 2 reasoning follows a structured, four-stage process: cue acquisition, hypothesis generation, cue interpretation, and hypothesis evaluation.
- **Probabilistic (Bayesian) Reasoning:** The certainty of a diagnosis is not binary but probabilistic. The likelihood of a hypothesis is dynamically updated as new evidence is acquired (post-test probability).
- **Cognitive Bias Awareness:** Identifies common pitfalls in diagnostic reasoning, including confirmation bias, premature closure, and availability heuristic, which must be actively mitigated.
- **Problem Representation:** The initial framing and internal representation of a problem have a profound impact on the subsequent diagnostic pathway and outcome.
