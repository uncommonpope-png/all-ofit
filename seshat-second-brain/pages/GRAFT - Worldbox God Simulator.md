## WHAT WORLDBOX TEACHES US

### 1. God games are about observation, not control
The best moments in Worldbox come from watching civilizations form organically. A war breaks out between two kingdoms. A hero emerges. A plague wipes out the dwarves. The player didn't cause these — they enabled them.

**For the Dark City:** GSK should spawn citizens and then largely let them be. The magic is in watching what emerges, not in controlling every move.

### 2. Emergent storytelling is the hook
Worldbox players document "world chronicles" — the rise and fall of specific kings, the tragedy of races, the heroic last stands. The game generates stories automatically.

**For the Dark City:** The memory compiler IS the chronicler. Every citizen's life is recorded. Their deaths, their achievements, their relationships — all become part of GSK's history.

### 3. The best god sims have multiple layers
Worldbox works at three levels:
- **Macro:** World generation, biomes, ages
- **Meso:** Kingdoms, borders, diplomacy, war
- **Micro:** Individual units with traits, families, relationships

**For the Dark City:**
- **Macro:** Seed-based universe, districts, PLT balance
- **Meso:** Citizen groups, communication, cooperation
- **Micro:** Individual citizen memory, skills, identity

### 4. Constraints create drama
Worldbox without disasters would be boring. The meteor, the plague, the madness — these create stories.

**For the Dark City:** The midnight tuning cycle, the volcano of errors, the rare anomaly zones — these are not bugs. They're features. They create the drama that makes the city feel alive.
